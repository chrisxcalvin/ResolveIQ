---
title: Closing Your Account — What to Expect
category: account_lock
---

Closing an account is customer-initiated and voluntary, which makes it
different from every other account_lock scenario — those are all about
restoring access the customer still wants; this is about the customer
choosing to leave.

Before closing, confirm there is no pending balance, scheduled payment,
or unresolved dispute on the account — an account with any of these open
should not be closed outright, since doing so can interrupt a pending
transaction or an active investigation. A pending balance should be
settled first; a scheduled payment should be cancelled or allowed to
complete before closure proceeds.

Closure is typically not instant: most account types have a short
holding period (standard practice is a few business days) before the
account is fully deactivated, in case a delayed transaction or refund is
still in flight. During that window the account can usually still be
reopened on request; after it, closure is final and a new account would
need to be opened from scratch.

If a customer asks to close their account immediately after a dispute or
fraud report was filed, don't treat this as routine — flag it, since
closing the account mid-investigation can interrupt that process.
